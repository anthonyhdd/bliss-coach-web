The best Yoodli alternative depends on what you're actually practicing: Yoodli is a speech coach that scores how you talk — filler words, pace, eye contact — while a mock-interview app like Alex runs the interview itself, asking follow-up questions and reacting to your answers. If your goal is a specific job interview, you want the second kind; if your goal is general public-speaking delivery, Yoodli is built for that.

## What Yoodli is good at

Yoodli records you speaking and gives analytics on delivery: how many filler words ("um," "like"), your talking pace, word choice, and conciseness. It's genuinely useful for presentations, sales calls, and reducing verbal tics. You speak, it measures, you review the report.

Where it's a weaker fit for **interview** prep specifically: it analyzes a monologue. A real interview is a conversation — the interviewer asks a question, listens, then digs into the weak part of your answer ("you said you led the project — what did *you* personally decide?"). Delivery analytics don't rehearse that pressure.

## Yoodli vs a conversational mock interviewer

| | Yoodli | Alex |
|---|---|---|
| Core job | Speech-delivery analytics | Runs the mock interview itself |
| Format | You speak, it scores the recording | Back-and-forth: it asks, follows up, reacts |
| Follow-up questions | No | Yes — probes weak answers |
| Company/role context | General | Tailors questions to the role and company |
| Best for | Presentations, filler-word reduction | Rehearsing an actual upcoming interview |
| STAR-answer coaching | Not the focus | Built around it |

## Who should pick which

**Pick Yoodli if** your problem is delivery — you ramble, say "um" constantly, or need to rehearse a set talk. Its analytics are the point.

**Pick a conversational mock interviewer (like [Alex](/alex/)) if** you have an interview coming up and want to *rehearse the interview*, not just measure your voice. Alex plays the recruiter, asks the questions that company actually asks, follows up on the vague parts of your answer, and coaches you into a tight [STAR structure](/alex/blog/how-to-introduce-yourself-in-an-interview/). You can practice out loud from your phone the night before, as many rounds as you want.

Plenty of people use both: Yoodli to clean up *how* they sound, a mock interviewer to sharpen *what* they say under follow-up.

## FAQ

**Is there a free Yoodli alternative?**
Most tools in this space, including Alex, let you try mock interviews before subscribing. Try one interview end to end — the real test is whether the follow-up questions feel like a real recruiter, not whether the landing page looks polished.

**Does Yoodli do mock interviews?**
Yoodli focuses on speech-delivery feedback on what you record. A dedicated mock-interview app instead drives the conversation — asking questions and reacting — which is closer to the pressure of the real thing.

**What's the best AI interview practice app?**
For rehearsing a specific interview, pick one that holds a real back-and-forth and tailors questions to your target role and company, then coaches your answer structure. For general speaking polish, a delivery analyzer like Yoodli is the better tool.
