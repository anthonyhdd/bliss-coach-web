If you like Praktika's idea — talking out loud to an AI instead of tapping multiple-choice — but want a Spanish-focused version, the thing to compare isn't features, it's the teacher. Praktika is a broad, multi-language AI conversation app. A single-language tutor like Sofia goes the other way: one language, one consistent teacher persona, and lessons built around actually speaking Mexican Spanish from the first session.

## Why "talk to an AI" is the right instinct

Traditional apps drill vocabulary you can recognize but can't produce under pressure. Speaking apps fix the real gap: forming sentences out loud, in real time, without freezing. Praktika popularized this for a reason — conversation reps are what move you from "I studied Spanish" to "I can hold a conversation."

So the question isn't whether to use a speaking app. It's which one keeps you talking.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Praktika if** you're learning several languages, or want to sample AI speaking practice broadly across a big catalog.

**Pick a dedicated Spanish tutor (like [Sofia](/sofia/)) if** Spanish is *the* goal and you want it to feel like a relationship with one teacher rather than a feature in a big app. Sofia speaks Mexican Spanish with you, remembers what you talked about last time, corrects you gently, and grades the conversation to your level — from complete beginner up. The narrower focus is the point: one teacher, one language, more reps in it.

## FAQ

**Is there a free Praktika alternative?**
Yes — most AI speaking apps, including Sofia, let you have real conversations before subscribing. Test the one thing that matters: does talking to it feel natural enough that you'd come back tomorrow?

**What's the best app to practice speaking Spanish with AI?**
For Spanish specifically, a single-language tutor tends to feel more coherent than a multi-language platform — one consistent teacher, Spanish-first lessons, and personalization that builds on your past conversations.

**Do AI speaking apps actually help you learn to speak?**
Yes, when they keep you talking. The gains come from producing the language out loud repeatedly with instant correction — which is exactly what conversation-based apps are built to give you, and what passive tapping never did.
