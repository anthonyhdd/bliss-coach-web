Short answer: be kind, be clear, and be brief. One or two sentences that thank them, say no without ambiguity, and wish them well: "I really enjoyed meeting you, but I didn't feel a romantic connection. I wish you the best." Kindness is in the tone, not in leaving the door half open.

## What's actually going on

Most people ghost because they're afraid a clear "no" will hurt more than silence. It's usually the reverse. Silence leaves the other person wondering, rereading, and sending the follow-up they'll later regret. A clear, warm no ends that loop in a minute.

The two traps are opposite:

- **Too vague.** "I'm just really busy right now" sounds kind but reads as "ask me again in two weeks." They will.
- **Too detailed.** Listing reasons invites debate. "You talk about your ex a lot" gives them something to argue with.

The sweet spot is one honest, general reason (or none) and a clear conclusion.

How much you owe someone scales with how much you've shared. A match you exchanged three messages with needs one line, or nothing at all. Someone you've been on three dates with deserves a proper text. Someone you've been seeing for weeks deserves a call.

## What NOT to send

- **"I'm not ready for anything right now"** — if it isn't true, they'll see you on the apps next week.
- **"Maybe we can be friends?"** — only offer this if you'd actually follow through.
- **"You're great, it's just…"** followed by a list of their flaws.
- **"lol no"** — even for a stranger, cruelty is optional.
- **A reply three days late with no acknowledgment.** If you're late, say so briefly.

## Copy-ready texts

### To a match you've only chatted with

> Hey, I've enjoyed chatting, but I don't think we're a match. Good luck out there!

> I don't feel the spark I'm looking for, so I'm going to step back. Wishing you the best.

> Thanks for the conversation! I'm going to pass, but I hope you find someone great.

### After one or two dates

> I had a nice time with you, but I didn't feel a romantic connection. I didn't want to leave you guessing. Take care!

> Thank you for dinner — I enjoyed it. I don't see this going further romantically, and I wanted to be upfront about it. All the best.

> I've thought about it and I don't think we're the right fit. You're easy to talk to and I'm sure the right person will see that.

### After several dates

> I've really enjoyed getting to know you, and that's why I want to be honest: I don't see this becoming a relationship. I'm sorry — I wanted to tell you clearly rather than drift away.

> This isn't easy to write. I like you as a person, but my feelings haven't grown the way I hoped. I think it's best that we stop seeing each other. Thank you for the time we spent together.

If you've been intimate or it's lasted more than a few weeks, consider a call instead, or offer one: "I'd rather say this properly — can I call you tonight?"

### To a friend who has feelings for you

> I really value our friendship, and I don't feel the same way romantically. I hope that's okay, and I'd love for us to stay friends if you want that too.

> Thank you for telling me — that took guts. I don't feel the same, but I care about you a lot as a friend.

### If they ask why

> There's nothing wrong with you. It's just not the connection I'm looking for.

> I don't have a better reason than that the chemistry wasn't there for me. I'm sorry.

### If they push back or get angry

> I understand you're disappointed. My answer won't change, but I wish you well.

Then stop replying. You don't owe a second round.

## Timing

- **Sooner is kinder.** Once you know, say it. Every extra day of mixed signals makes it harder to hear.
- **Reply before the next plan is made.** If they're asking about Saturday, answer before Saturday.
- **Daytime, not late at night.** Nobody wants to read a rejection at midnight.

## When to stop

After your message, one polite reply is enough if they answer graciously ("Thanks for being honest!" — "Of course. Take care!"). If they respond with anger or keep pushing, you can stop engaging. If they become abusive, block — your safety comes first.

## FAQ

**Is it rude to reject someone over text?**
No. For matches and early dating, text is normal and often preferred — it lets them react privately. After several weeks together, a call or in-person conversation is more respectful.

**Is ghosting better than a rejection text?**
For someone you've only messaged a few times, silence is common. Once you've met in person, a short, kind text is better — it ends the uncertainty.

**How do you reject someone without hurting them?**
You can't remove all disappointment, but you can avoid adding to it: be prompt, brief, clear, and don't list their flaws.

**Should I give a reason when turning someone down?**
A general one is enough ("I didn't feel a romantic connection"). Specific criticisms invite debate and hurt more than they help.

**What if they keep texting after I said no?**
Repeat once, briefly, that your answer won't change, then stop replying. Block if they become aggressive.
