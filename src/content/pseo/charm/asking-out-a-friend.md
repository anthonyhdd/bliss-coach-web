Short answer: make it unmistakably a date, keep it light, and give them an easy exit. "I'd like to take you out — like, on a date. Dinner Friday? And if that's not your thing, no weirdness, I promise." The word *date* does the heavy lifting. Without it, you'll end up at another hangout wondering what just happened.

## What's actually going on

Asking out a friend is scary for one reason: there's something to lose. That fear pushes most people toward vague invites — "we should get dinner sometime" — which your friend will almost always read as a normal friend plan. Now you're on a date they don't know is a date, and nobody can relax.

Clarity is the kind option. It lets your friend answer the real question, and it protects the friendship far better than months of hints.

Before you send anything, check for a few signals:

- Do they make one-on-one time with you, not just group time?
- Do they text you first, and about more than logistics?
- Is there physical ease — sitting close, lingering hugs?
- Do they mention other people they're dating? (If yes, and often, that's a signal too.)

None of these guarantee a yes, but if none are there, prepare for a warm no.

## What NOT to send

- **"We should hang out sometime!"** Reads as friendship. You'll get friendship.
- **A long confession text.** Three paragraphs about how long you've felt this way puts enormous pressure on the reply.
- **Asking through a mutual friend first.** It turns a private question into group news.
- **"Hypothetically, would you ever date someone like me?"** Hypotheticals make people uncomfortable, not honest.
- **Asking while drunk.** If it goes badly, they'll wonder whether you meant it.

## Copy-ready texts

### Direct and warm

> I've been wanting to ask you this for a while: would you want to go on a date with me? A real one. Dinner Saturday?

> I like you as more than a friend. Would you want to go out sometime and see where it goes? Zero pressure either way.

> Okay, I'm just going to say it — I'd love to take you on a date. If you're not into it, we're completely fine, and I'll still steal your fries.

### Lighter, still unmistakable

> New idea: instead of our usual Thursday thing, what if we made it a date? You, me, the Italian place, candles and everything.

> Would it be weird if I asked you out? Because I'm asking you out.

> I've had a crush on you for a while, and I'd kick myself if I never asked. Drinks this week?

### When you want to say it in person instead

> Are you free for a walk tomorrow? There's something I'd like to talk to you about — nothing bad, promise.

Only use this if you'll actually say it on the walk. "Nothing bad, promise" matters; otherwise they'll spend the night worrying.

### If they say yes

> Honestly, relieved and very happy. Saturday at 7?

Lock in a time and place in the same conversation.

### If they say no

> Thanks for being honest — I really appreciate it. We're good, and I'm still counting on you for Thursday.

> Totally understand. Nothing changes on my side. See you at [usual plan]?

Then prove it: show up to the next normal plan and be yourself. The friendship survives when *you* make it clear that it does.

### If they say "I don't know"

> That's fair, take your time. No rush, and no weirdness either way.

Don't push for an answer that day.

## Timing

- **Pick a calm moment,** not the middle of a group chat, a crisis, or right after they've mentioned a breakup.
- **Daytime or early evening** so they can reply thoughtfully.
- **Don't ask right before a big shared event** (a trip, a wedding) where a no would make things awkward for days.

## When to stop

If they say no, don't ask again unless they bring it up. Give it some space in the friendship too — a normal plan within a week or two, not daily check-ins.

If they say yes, treat the first date like any first date: a clear plan, a short confirmation the day before (see [how to confirm a date over text](/charm/what-to-text/confirming-a-date/)), and no pressure to define things right away.

## FAQ

**Should I ask a friend out over text or in person?**
Either works. Text gives them room to think and answer honestly; in person is warmer but puts them on the spot. If you text, make it clearly a date.

**How do I ask a friend out without making it awkward?**
Be clear, keep it short, and say explicitly that the friendship is fine if they're not interested — then act like it.

**How do I know if my friend likes me back?**
Look for one-on-one time, messages that go beyond logistics, and physical ease. No single sign is proof, which is why asking clearly beats guessing.

**Can a friendship survive a rejected date request?**
Usually, yes — especially if you ask kindly, accept the answer gracefully, and keep showing up as a friend.

**What if my friend is dating someone?**
Don't ask. Wait until they're single, or accept that the moment isn't there.
