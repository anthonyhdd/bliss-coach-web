Short answer: short, warm, and pressure-free. "Sorry you're feeling rough. Rest up — no need to reply. Want me to drop off soup?" One kind message and one concrete offer beat a stream of check-ins.

## What's actually going on

When someone's sick, they have little energy for conversation. The best text makes her feel thought of without asking her to do anything. That's why "no need to reply" is so powerful: it turns your message into a gift instead of a task.

How far to go depends on where you are:

- **Early dating (a few dates):** keep it light. A kind text and maybe a funny one. Showing up at her door uninvited is too much.
- **Seeing each other regularly:** an offer to bring something is thoughtful — let her decide.
- **In a relationship:** actions count more than words. Soup, medicine, a movie on the sofa if she wants company.

## What NOT to send

- **"Are you really sick or just avoiding me?"** Even as a joke.
- **"Call me when you're better"** with a hint of annoyance about cancelled plans.
- **Hourly "how are you feeling?" messages.** Exhausting to answer.
- **Unsolicited medical advice** or a list of remedies.
- **"Can I still come over tonight?"** Let her lead.

## Copy-ready texts

### Early dating

> So sorry you're sick! Hope you feel better soon. Rest up — Friday can wait.

> Sending get-well vibes and an imaginary bowl of soup. No need to reply.

> That's rough, I'm sorry. Take care of yourself and let me know when you're back on your feet.

### When you're closer

> Want me to drop off soup and tissues? I'll leave them at the door, no need to get out of bed.

> Thinking of you. Is there anything you need from the pharmacy?

> I'm free tonight if you want company for a very low-energy movie. Totally fine if you'd rather sleep.

### In a relationship

> I'm grabbing you some ginger tea and your favourite crackers. Be there in 30.

> You don't need to do anything today. I'll handle dinner.

> I miss you, but mostly I want you to feel better. Rest and text me if you need anything at all.

### Funny (if she has the energy for it)

> Your immune system clearly didn't get the memo about our plans. I'm filing a complaint.

> Official diagnosis from Dr. Me: needs soup, sleep, and a truly bad reality show.

> Get well soon — the world is weirdly quiet without your sarcasm.

### Checking in the next day

> Morning! Feeling any better?

> Hope today is a little easier. Still happy to bring anything by.

## Timing

- **Reply right away** when she tells you she's sick. A quick, caring answer matters more than the perfect wording.
- **One check-in a day** is plenty. More than that becomes pressure.
- **If she cancels plans because she's sick,** don't ask to reschedule in the same message. Suggest a new date once she's better.

## When to stop

If she doesn't reply, she's probably resting — let her. One message a day is the maximum.

When she's feeling better, suggest a low-key plan to make up for the missed one. ## FAQ

**Should I text her when she's sick?**
Yes — a short, caring message shows you're thinking of her. Just make it clear she doesn't need to reply.

**How often should I check on her?**
Once a day is usually right. More than that can feel like pressure when she's low on energy.

**Is it too much to bring her soup?**
Offer first, especially early on. In a relationship, showing up with something she likes is usually welcome.

**What if she's too sick to text back?**
That's normal. Don't take it personally, and don't send more messages asking why she hasn't replied.

**What's a sweet get-well text for a crush?**
Something short and warm with no expectations: "Hope you feel better soon — rest up, no need to reply."
