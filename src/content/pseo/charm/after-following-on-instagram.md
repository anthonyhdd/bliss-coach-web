Short answer: reply to a story, not a post from three years ago. Stories are designed for replies, so a comment there feels natural: "Okay, where is this beach? It looks unreal." One specific, curious line beats any compliment about her looks.

## What's actually going on

A DM from someone new can feel like a nice surprise or an intrusion, and the difference is almost entirely about context and tone.

- **Story replies** feel natural. She posted something and invited reactions.
- **Comments on old posts** feel like you've been scrolling her profile for an hour. You probably have, but don't show it.
- **Cold "hey beautiful" DMs** go straight to the requests folder and stay there.

It also matters how you're connected. If you met in person first, or share friends, a DM is easy. If she's a complete stranger, keep expectations modest — many people ignore DMs from people they don't know, no matter how well written.

## What NOT to send

- **"Hey beautiful"** / **"Hi gorgeous."** Hundreds of people send this.
- **Fire emojis on every story.** It reads as low effort.
- **Liking ten old photos in a row.** It says "I went deep."
- **A voice note as a first message.** Too much, too soon.
- **Anything about her body.** Instant block for good reason.
- **A second DM when the first has no reply.** One is enough.

## Copy-ready texts

### Story replies

> Okay, I need the name of this café immediately.

> Is that the new exhibition at the museum? Worth going?

> This hike looks amazing. How long did it take?

> Your dog is clearly the main character here.

> That playlist choice is elite. What's the song?

### If you met in person first

> Hey! It's Leo from the climbing gym. Glad I found you on here — still recovering from that route you made look easy.

> Great meeting you at Mark's birthday! Following up on our debate about the best bagel in town.

### If you have mutual friends

> Hey! I think we both know Sarah — I've seen you in her photos from the lake trip. Your story about the canoe had me laughing.

### Light compliment (only once there's some back-and-forth)

> You have excellent taste in basically everything you post.

> You make the rest of us look like we're not even trying on Instagram.

### Moving from DMs to a date

> This is fun, but I'd rather hear these stories in person. Coffee this week?

> Want to swap Instagram for a real drink? I know a place with a view like your story.

> Can I get your number? I'm better at texting than DMs.

## Timing

- **Reply to stories while they're fresh**, ideally within a few hours of posting.
- **Don't DM at 2am.** Late-night DMs from strangers have a reputation.
- **Suggest meeting or swapping numbers after a few good exchanges.** DMs are a starting point, not the place to get to know someone for weeks.

## When to stop

If she doesn't reply, don't follow up — and don't start reacting to every story either. If she replies briefly without asking anything back, let the conversation rest.

If it's going well, move it off Instagram quickly. ## FAQ

**Is it weird to DM someone you don't know on Instagram?**
It's common, but many people ignore strangers' DMs. Your odds are much better if you've met or share friends.

**What's the best way to start a conversation on Instagram?**
Reply to a story with a specific, curious comment or question about what she shared.

**Should I like her photos before I DM?**
A couple of recent likes is fine. Liking a long run of old posts looks intense.

**How long should I DM before asking for her number?**
After a few good exchanges — often the same day. Suggest a number or a meetup before it becomes a pen-pal situation.

**What if she leaves my DM on seen?**
Treat it as a no. Don't send another message or react to all her stories.
