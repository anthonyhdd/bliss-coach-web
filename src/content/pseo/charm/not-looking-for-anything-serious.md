Short answer: take her at her word, then be honest about what *you* want. If you want the same, say so simply: "Same here — let's just have fun and see." If you want more, don't pretend otherwise: "I appreciate you being upfront. I'm looking for something more, so I don't think we want the same thing."

## What's actually going on

"I'm not looking for anything serious" is one of the clearest things someone can say in dating, and one of the most frequently ignored. It can mean:

- **She genuinely wants something casual** right now — no labels, no future plans.
- **She's just out of something** and isn't ready for commitment.
- **She's not sure about you** and is keeping expectations low.
- **She's protecting herself** because she's been hurt before.

You can't know which one it is from one text, and you don't need to. What matters is whether what she wants matches what you want.

The trap is agreeing to casual while secretly hoping to change her mind. Sometimes that works; much more often, it ends with one person hurt.

## What NOT to send

- **"That's fine, I'm chill"** — if you're not.
- **"You'll change your mind once you get to know me."** Pressure dressed as confidence.
- **"Why not? What's wrong with serious?"** Makes her defend her choice.
- **Going silent.** If it's a dealbreaker, say so.
- **"So you just want a hookup?"** Don't put words in her mouth.

## Copy-ready texts

### If you want the same

> Honestly, same. Let's keep it easy and enjoy it.

> Works for me. No pressure either way — I just like spending time with you.

> Totally fine. Let's see where it goes without overthinking it.

### If you're open but unsure

> I appreciate you telling me. I'm not in a rush either, but I'd like to keep getting to know you and see how it feels.

> Good to know. I'm open to keeping it relaxed for now — if either of us wants something different later, let's just say so.

The second one sets up an honest check-in later without pushing now.

### If you want something serious

> Thanks for being honest. I'm looking for a relationship, so I don't think we're on the same page. I really enjoyed our time together, though.

> I respect that. I'm at a point where I want something more, so I'm going to step back. No hard feelings at all.

> I'm glad you said it early. I'm hoping for something longer-term, so I'll bow out — but it was great getting to know you.

### If you're not sure what she means

> Can I ask what that looks like for you? Just so we're on the same page.

> Does that mean dating other people too, or just not putting a label on it?

Asking what "not serious" means is fair, as long as you ask once and listen to the answer.

### Later, if feelings change

> I know we said we'd keep it casual, but I've started to feel more than that. I wanted to be honest. How do you feel?

## Timing

- **Reply when you've actually thought about what you want.** A quick "haha cool" you'll regret is worse than waiting an hour.
- **If it's a dealbreaker, say so soon.** Waiting months makes it harder.

## When to stop

If you want different things, the kindest thing for both of you is to step back cleanly. Don't keep texting in the hope she changes her mind.

If you both want something casual, check in honestly from time to time. ## FAQ

**What does it mean when a girl says she's not looking for anything serious?**
Usually exactly that: she doesn't want commitment right now. The reason varies, but the safest move is to believe her.

**Can "not looking for anything serious" turn into a relationship?**
Sometimes, but don't count on it. If you need a relationship, say so instead of waiting for her to change.

**How do I respond if I want more?**
Thank her for being honest and say clearly that you're looking for something serious, then step back.

**Is it okay to ask what she means?**
Yes — once. Ask what it looks like for her, listen, and decide if it works for you.

**Should I stop talking to her?**
If you want different things and it will hurt you to stay, yes. If you're both comfortable with something casual, there's no need.
