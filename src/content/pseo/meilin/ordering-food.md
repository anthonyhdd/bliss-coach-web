To order food in Chinese, point and say **我要这个** (*wǒ yào zhège*, "I want this one"). That single phrase will feed you anywhere in China. Add **不要辣** (*bú yào là*, "not spicy") and **买单** (*mǎidān*, "the bill, please"), and you can get through almost any meal. Below are the phrases in the order you'll need them, with the tone that most learners get wrong.

## Getting the server's attention

- **服务员！** — *fúwùyuán!* — "Server!" This is normal and polite in China; don't feel rude calling it out.
- **你好，** — *nǐ hǎo* — "Hello," works as a softer way to get attention.
- **请给我菜单。** — *qǐng gěi wǒ càidān* — "Please give me the menu."

Many restaurants now have you scan a QR code to order: **扫码点餐** (*sǎo mǎ diǎn cān*, "scan to order"). If you see it on the table, that's what it means.

Tone tip: *fúwùyuán* is rising–falling–rising (2-4-2). Say *fú* like a question, *wù* like a firm "no," then *yuán* rising again.

## Ordering

- **我要这个。** — *wǒ yào zhège* — "I want this one." Point at the menu or at someone else's plate.
- **我要一个……** — *wǒ yào yí gè…* — "I'd like one…"
- **有什么推荐的？** — *yǒu shénme tuījiàn de?* — "What do you recommend?"
- **你们的招牌菜是什么？** — *nǐmen de zhāopái cài shì shénme?* — "What's your signature dish?"
- **这个是什么？** — *zhège shì shénme?* — "What is this?"
- **再来一碗米饭。** — *zài lái yì wǎn mǐfàn* — "Another bowl of rice, please."
- **就这些。** — *jiù zhèxiē* — "That's all."

Tone tip: *yào* (want) is a sharp fourth tone, falling. If it drifts upward, it can sound like *yáo* (to shake).

## Spice, allergies and diets

- **不要辣。** — *bú yào là* — "Not spicy." Note that 不 changes to *bú* (rising) before a fourth tone.
- **微辣。** — *wēi là* — "Mildly spicy."
- **少放辣。** — *shǎo fàng là* — "Go easy on the chilli."
- **少放盐。** — *shǎo fàng yán* — "Less salt."
- **我吃素。** — *wǒ chī sù* — "I'm vegetarian."
- **我对花生过敏。** — *wǒ duì huāshēng guòmǐn* — "I'm allergic to peanuts."
- **不要香菜。** — *bú yào xiāngcài* — "No coriander." Many people will be glad you asked.

Be careful: in Sichuan or Hunan, *wēi là* can still be very hot by most standards. If you really can't take spice, say *bú yào là* and add **一点都不要** (*yìdiǎn dōu bú yào*, "none at all").

## During the meal

- **很好吃！** — *hěn hǎochī!* — "It's delicious!"
- **可以加点水吗？** — *kěyǐ jiā diǎn shuǐ ma?* — "Could I have some more water?"
- **可以打包吗？** — *kěyǐ dǎbāo ma?* — "Can I take this to go?"

## Paying

- **买单。** — *mǎidān* — "The bill, please." In the south you'll also hear **埋单** (*máidān*).
- **多少钱？** — *duōshao qián?* — "How much is it?"
- **可以扫码吗？** — *kěyǐ sǎo mǎ ma?* — "Can I pay by scanning a code?" Mobile payment is the norm.
- **可以刷卡吗？** — *kěyǐ shuā kǎ ma?* — "Can I pay by card?"

Tipping isn't customary in mainland China, so you don't need a phrase for it.

## A sample exchange

> **服务员：** 你好，几位？ (*Nǐ hǎo, jǐ wèi?* — Hello, how many?)
> **You:** 两位。 (*Liǎng wèi.* — Two.)
> **服务员：** 想吃点什么？ (*Xiǎng chī diǎn shénme?* — What would you like?)
> **You:** 我要这个，不要辣。再来两碗米饭。 (*Wǒ yào zhège, bú yào là. Zài lái liǎng wǎn mǐfàn.*)
> **服务员：** 好的。 (*Hǎo de.* — Okay.)

Notice *liǎng* (两) instead of *èr* (二) for "two" when counting things or people.

## FAQ

**How do you say "I want this" in Chinese?**
我要这个 — *wǒ yào zhège*. Point at the dish as you say it; it works in any restaurant.

**How do you say "not spicy" in Chinese?**
不要辣 — *bú yào là*. For mild, say 微辣 — *wēi là*.

**How do you ask for the bill in a Chinese restaurant?**
Say 买单 — *mǎidān* — or wave and say 服务员，买单 (*fúwùyuán, mǎidān*).

**Is it rude to call "fúwùyuán" out loud?**
No. It's the normal way to get a server's attention in China.

**How do you say "I'm vegetarian" in Mandarin?**
我吃素 — *wǒ chī sù*. You can add 不要肉 (*bú yào ròu*, "no meat") to be extra clear.
