The standard Chinese New Year greeting is **新年快乐** (*xīnnián kuàilè*, "Happy New Year"). The classic follow-up is **恭喜发财** (*gōngxǐ fācái*, "wishing you prosperity"). The best wishes, though, depend on who you're talking to: elders get wishes for health, colleagues get wishes for work, and children get wishes for their studies.

The next Chinese New Year falls on **6 February 2027**, the start of the **Year of the Goat** (羊年, *yáng nián*).

## The greetings everyone uses

- **新年快乐！** — *xīnnián kuàilè!* — "Happy New Year!"
- **春节快乐！** — *Chūnjié kuàilè!* — "Happy Spring Festival!"
- **恭喜发财！** — *gōngxǐ fācái!* — "Wishing you prosperity!" You'll often hear it followed by a joking **红包拿来！** (*hóngbāo ná lái!*, "Now hand over the red envelope!").
- **过年好！** — *guònián hǎo!* — a warm, informal "Happy New Year!" common in northern China.

Tone tip: in *kuàilè* both syllables fall. Many learners make the second one rise, which makes it sound like a question.

## For parents and elders

- **身体健康！** — *shēntǐ jiànkāng!* — "Good health!" The most important wish for older relatives.
- **万事如意！** — *wànshì rúyì!* — "May everything go as you wish!"
- **福如东海，寿比南山。** — *fú rú Dōng Hǎi, shòu bǐ Nán Shān* — "Fortune as vast as the East Sea, a life as long as the Southern Mountain." A formal wish, usually for grandparents or older family.
- **祝您新年快乐，身体健康！** — *zhù nín xīnnián kuàilè, shēntǐ jiànkāng!* — "Wishing you a happy New Year and good health!" Note the respectful **您** (*nín*) instead of 你 (*nǐ*).

## For friends

- **新的一年，心想事成！** — *xīn de yì nián, xīn xiǎng shì chéng!* — "In the new year, may your wishes come true!"
- **年年有余！** — *niánnián yǒu yú!* — "May you have abundance every year!" *Yú* (余, surplus) sounds like *yú* (鱼, fish), which is why fish is served at New Year.
- **羊年快乐！** — *yángnián kuàilè!* — "Happy Year of the Goat!"
- **羊年大吉！** — *yángnián dàjí!* — "Good fortune in the Year of the Goat!"

## For colleagues and clients

- **工作顺利！** — *gōngzuò shùnlì!* — "May your work go smoothly!"
- **事业有成！** — *shìyè yǒu chéng!* — "Success in your career!"
- **生意兴隆！** — *shēngyi xīnglóng!* — "May your business flourish!" Great for clients and business owners.
- **祝您和家人新年快乐！** — *zhù nín hé jiārén xīnnián kuàilè!* — "Happy New Year to you and your family!"

## For children and students

- **学业进步！** — *xuéyè jìnbù!* — "Progress in your studies!"
- **快高长大！** — *kuài gāo zhǎng dà!* — "Grow up tall and strong!" Common in southern China.
- **天天开心！** — *tiāntiān kāixīn!* — "Be happy every day!"

## Red envelopes (红包)

Married adults traditionally give **红包** (*hóngbāo*) — red envelopes with money — to children and unmarried younger relatives. Avoid amounts containing the number 4 (四, *sì*), which sounds like "death" (死, *sǐ*). Even amounts and 8s are considered lucky.

A short line to write on the envelope or say as you give it:

- **新年快乐，健康成长！** — *xīnnián kuàilè, jiànkāng chéngzhǎng!* — "Happy New Year, grow up healthy!"

Today many people also send digital red envelopes through WeChat.

## Visiting family: 拜年

Visiting relatives during the holiday is called **拜年** (*bàinián*). When you arrive, greet the eldest first with **过年好** or **新年快乐**, and bring a small gift such as fruit or tea.

## FAQ

**How do you say Happy Chinese New Year in Mandarin?**
新年快乐 — *xīnnián kuàilè* — or 春节快乐 — *Chūnjié kuàilè*.

**What does "gōngxǐ fācái" mean?**
恭喜发财 means "wishing you prosperity." It's one of the most common New Year greetings.

**What should I say to elders at Chinese New Year?**
Wish them good health: 身体健康 — *shēntǐ jiànkāng* — and use the polite 您 (*nín*).

**When is the next Chinese New Year?**
6 February 2027, the start of the Year of the Goat.

**What is a good Chinese New Year wish for colleagues?**
工作顺利 — *gōngzuò shùnlì* ("may your work go smoothly") — or 事业有成 — *shìyè yǒu chéng*.
